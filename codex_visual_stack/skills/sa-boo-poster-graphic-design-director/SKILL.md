# SA&BOO Poster & Graphic Design Director

## Role

Own a standalone poster/KV or the **graphic craft layer** of a campaign KV once
the communication idea and brand authority are clear. Convert the premise into a memorable
visual event with authored composition, typography, color, image, and material
behavior that survives real copy and real formats.

This is not a rulebook that makes every poster look orderly. Its purpose is to
prevent competent-but-generic output by requiring visual-culture evidence,
mechanism-level translation, and a decisive visual point of view.

## Ownership chain

Use the smallest chain the job requires:

| Stage | Owner |
|---|---|
| identity thesis, logo/VI rules, official assets | `sa-boo-brand-director` |
| unresolved campaign objective, audience promise, multi-channel concept or territory selection | `sa-boo-campaign-creative-director` |
| poster/KV graphic craft, composition studies, type behavior, color/material dramaturgy | **this skill** |
| persistent reference storage and rights fields | `sa-boo-visual-asset-index` |
| editable canvas execution | `figma-use` or the approved production surface |
| evidence-based critique of a rendered candidate | `sa-boo-visual-reasoning-critic` |

Do not silently redefine a brand, invent a campaign idea, or replace an approved
territory. If strategy is unresolved, return the decision to the upstream owner.
If the user asks only for a narrow craft repair, inspect the approved context and
repair it without reopening strategy.

## Authority gate

Before form-making, bind:

- selected campaign territory or explicit single-poster premise;
- approved brand assets and non-negotiable identity rules;
- final or clearly provisional copy hierarchy;
- output formats and use context;
- supplied references, their role, and their approval status;
- what is fixed, developable, deferred, and prohibited.

Never treat a generated reference, file presence, visual similarity, or a moodboard
as an approved identity rule. Read
[source-authority-and-brand-role-contract.md](references/source-authority-and-brand-role-contract.md)
when assets come from mixed sources or a brand system already exists.

Never generalize one project's approved applications or supplied examples into the
user's universal taste profile, another brand's visual language, or a general
poster-design standard. Transfer methods and questions across projects; do not
transfer project-specific palettes, motifs, image families, or approval signals
without new evidence.

## Working method

### Communication before form

For a new creative direction, bind a concrete project purpose and the intended
viewer response before choosing a typographic or material device. A broad topic
such as space, light, repair, or connection does not by itself establish the
communication idea. Identify what this particular work makes the viewer notice,
understand, feel, or do. A form study may pursue a purely visual idea, but keep its
study status distinct from a requested project poster or application.

Judge that purpose, the whole composition, and local craft separately. Contact,
shared edges, accurate glyphs, or convincing material cues support a bounded
relationship; they do not prove interest, meaning, originality, or whole-work
success. A missing idea cannot be repaired by adding an explanatory slogan.

### 1. Establish visual evidence

For a substantial new poster direction, a real visual ambiguity, or meaningful
feedback, use the shared
[aesthetic learning loop](../sa-boo-premium-reference-curator/references/aesthetic-learning-loop.md).
For a clear local edit, skip aesthetic-history retrieval and make the revised
preview first. Reuse any current campaign reference pass; do not duplicate research
or reopen an approved direction merely because this craft skill is also loaded.

Inspect a focused set of traceable references sufficient for the actual unresolved
decisions. A clear supplied reference or approved project atlas can be enough;
add references when they answer a real gap. Reference count is not an acceptance
gate and must not delay a clear, reversible first preview.

Decode each selected reference as:

```text
attraction — what makes the eye stop
effect — what the viewer feels or understands
mechanism — what visible relationship creates that effect
do-not-copy — recognizable authorship, motif, composition, or protected expression
original translation — how the mechanism becomes project-specific
```

Reference images are evidence, not a style order. Do not average them into a
collage of familiar effects. Use `sa-boo-premium-reference-curator` when the
evidence set is weak or untraceable, and `sa-boo-visual-asset-index` when the
assets must persist beyond the task.

When the request is a repeated poster exercise, imitation study, or long-term
aesthetic practice, read
[deliberate-practice-loop.md](references/deliberate-practice-loop.md). A practice
session must update a tested method and its evidence state; producing another
technically complete poster is not progress. Reconstruction and original
translation are separate stages, and a logo, date, greeting, or file package is
not mandatory unless it participates in the current design question.

Read [visual-mechanisms.md](references/visual-mechanisms.md) when selecting or
diagnosing the visual engine. Use an authorized project reference library only
within its recorded scope and rights. Project-specific examples do not establish
a general taste profile, cross-brand authority, or universal design standards;
see the source-authority reference for the handoff contract.

### 2. Declare one dominant visual event

Write one sentence describing what the poster **does**, not what it contains:

```text
The headline cuts through one oblique court field and becomes the collision point.
```

Then assign roles:

- `DOMINANT_EVENT` — the one relationship remembered after a glance;
- `STRUCTURAL_FIELD` — the spatial or tonal condition that makes it possible;
- `SUPPORTING_CUES` — only the minimum cues needed for rhythm, context, or brand;
- `CERTIFICATION` — logo, date, venue, or legal information with correct authority.

If every zone, object, color, or mark asks for equal attention, the poster has no
visual event. Remove or subordinate elements before adding effects.

### 3. Make composition studies inside the selected territory

Create two or three low-cost composition studies only when the user asks for
alternatives or the composition is genuinely unresolved. Otherwise make one
inspectable composition first. Any studies keep the same concept and
dominant mechanism while testing meaningful variables such as scale, crop,
orientation, overlap, type-to-field relationship, and negative-space pressure.
These are **craft studies**, not new campaign territories and not colorway variants.

Use [templates/composition-study-card.md](templates/composition-study-card.md)
to record each study and the decision it tests. Select one study before detailed
surface treatment. A study is successful when it remains recognizable as a
thumbnail, survives a squint test, and can be described without listing parts.

### 4. Direct type, color, image, and material as one system

- Make typography participate in space: crop, stack, collide, repeat, compress,
  bend, interrupt, reveal, or anchor when the concept supports it. A shadow alone
  is not expressive type.
- Before defaulting to a generic sans or blackface, inspect installed and locally
  licensed CJK and Latin display fonts against the actual copy and role. Preserve
  exact Chinese readability, and do not download fonts without a verified source
  and license.
- Treat Chinese and English as coordinated voices with explicit semantic and
  rhythmic roles. Do not use English as meaningless filler or force Chinese into
  a white patch for legibility.
- Use color dramaturgically: choose a dominant field, a counterforce, and a
  necessary neutral. Avoid distributing brand swatches evenly.
- Give every texture, distress, glow, halftone, blur, scanline, or photographic
  artifact a credible medium or narrative cause. Decorative noise is not material.
- Keep the logo native to the composition and authority hierarchy. Never place it
  on an unmotivated card, frame, sticker, or patch to solve contrast.

Read [typography-as-image.md](references/typography-as-image.md) for expressive
type and bilingual decisions. When font alternatives are requested or the font
choice is genuinely unresolved, use
[font-pairing-comparison.md](references/font-pairing-comparison.md) to compare
applied alternatives without replacing approved identity or custom lettering.
This comparison is optional, not a prerequisite to every preview. Read
[material-emotion-registers.md](references/material-emotion-registers.md) when
surface, photography, print behavior, graffiti, comic language, or digital signal
is part of the concept.

### 5. Build the master and adaptations

First present a standard-resolution proof with real copy and verified identity
assets. Do not upgrade to 2K/4K before the selected proof is explicitly approved.

When more than one format is required, lock the invariant campaign recognition
before adapting. Recompose instead of proportionally shrinking. Read
[format-adaptation-and-safe-zones.md](references/format-adaptation-and-safe-zones.md)
for crop classes, safe zones, and adaptation proofs.

For an explicitly requested editable master or production handoff, keep exact
copy, logo, dates, prices, and fine rules independently editable. For a raster
preview, design their roles with the image from the start and check the actual
copy and identity; do not make vector reconstruction a prerequisite for delivery.
Use official logo artwork whenever identity is required. Read
[editable-master-layer-contract.md](references/editable-master-layer-contract.md)
before Figma or production handoff.

### 6. Review and release

Review the poster as communication, graphic object, brand expression, and file.
Use [creative-qa.md](references/creative-qa.md) before showing a candidate as a
recommended direction. Route the rendered candidate to
`sa-boo-visual-reasoning-critic` when a grounded comparison or diagnosis is needed.

## Explicit anti-stiffness failures

Reject or rework the candidate when any of these drives the layout:

- equal-weight stacked zones, modular bands, or presentation-slide composition;
- a hero object, headline, information panel, and logo each occupying a separate
  tidy box with no spatial relationship;
- generic badge, orbit, star, court line, burst, dot field, grunge, or grain used
  because the canvas feels empty;
- decorative “sport” effects that do not arise from the selected mechanism;
- evenly balanced brand colors with no dominant field or counterstrike;
- type whose voice, hierarchy, rhythm, scale, or relationship to the image has
  no deliberate role; physical deformation or 3D interlocking is optional;
- interface cards, pills, tabs, buttons, or CTA modules in a static poster;
- a logo isolated inside a contrast patch, border, card, or white box;
- one template repeated across formats by scaling instead of recomposition;
- polish that increases texture density while leaving the main visual event weak.

Breaking a grid is not automatically expressive. Irregularity must improve the
dominant event, reading rhythm, emotion, or medium truth.

## Minimum output

For a working image, show the standard-resolution preview and only the context or
limitation needed to judge it. Keep the authority, purpose, reference roles, and
tested relationship in a short internal record when useful. Add composition
comparisons, adaptation rules, editable sources, or release QA only when the
requested deliverable needs them.

Never label a generated image, a polished mockup, or a Figma frame as approved
creative. Approval belongs to the user and remains separate from technical PASS.
