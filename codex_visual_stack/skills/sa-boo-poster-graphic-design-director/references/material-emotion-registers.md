# Material and Emotion Registers

Read this when a direction uses print texture, photography, graffiti, comic language,
distress, glow, blur, halftone, collage, or digital effects. Choose a credible
register rather than combining all available treatments.

## Medium truth

Surface treatment must answer three questions:

1. **What medium or process could create this evidence?**
2. **How does that evidence support the campaign emotion?**
3. **Where should the effect stop so hierarchy remains clear?**

Texture is not a synonym for richness. A clean poster can have medium truth through
ink-like color, precise overprint relationships, photographic light, or a disciplined
digital signal. A noisy poster can still feel synthetic when artifacts have no cause.

## Registers

### Printed athletic field

Visible cues: large color planes, court paint, screenprint or offset character,
slight ink spread, controlled surface wear, hard white linework, decisive crop.

Emotional use: confident, physical, club-rooted, familiar without becoming nostalgic.

Control: let scale, paint, and color lead. Keep scratches and grain localized to
material evidence; do not cover the whole image with one grunge overlay.

### Editorial typographic object

Visible cues: authored letter scale, careful figure/ground, asymmetric pressure,
measured white or black field, precise microtype, one surprising spatial relation.

Emotional use: intelligent, international, culturally assured.

Control: minimal does not mean empty. The visual event must remain strong enough to
avoid a presentation-slide or luxury-template result.

### Comic impact

Visible cues: compressed action axis, burst or speed structure, hard silhouette,
halftone with directional logic, bold crop, exaggerated scale.

Emotional use: youth, wit, instant energy, collective excitement.

Control: build one panel-like event. Avoid distributing stars, dots, rays, and speech
devices evenly as category decoration.

### Site-specific graffiti or hand inscription

Visible cues: paint absorption, drips, overspray, marker pressure, wall joints,
perspective, ambient light, imperfect baseline, physical obstruction.

Emotional use: ownership, locality, community, defiance, lived culture.

Control: lettering must respond to the site. A clean logo pasted on a wall image is
not graffiti or material integration. Respect legality, site rights, and authorship.

### Photographic court or street document

Visible cues: credible place, real perspective, lighting, grain or motion from the
camera, observed human or environmental detail, strong crop.

Emotional use: authenticity, memory, aspiration, collective belonging.

Control: do not let stock-photo literalness flatten the campaign. Use sequencing,
crop, tonal separation, interruption, or type interaction with a clear reason.

### Digital signal and cyber court

Visible cues: scanline, glow, perspective grid, signal tear, chromatic offset, motion
trail, data frame, deep-field contrast.

Emotional use: night energy, broadcast, game-day intensity, future-facing tempo.

Control: choose one signal failure or transmission behavior. Avoid generic esports
frames, HUD furniture, buttons, and decorative technical numerals.

### Monochrome conceptual space

Visible cues: extreme value control, court geometry reduced to spatial line, hard
light, void, shadow, surreal scale, restrained type.

Emotional use: seriousness, suspense, ambition, a larger world beyond the game.

Control: black is not automatically premium. The concept must create spatial tension;
otherwise the result becomes merely dark and empty.

### Cut-and-paste collage

Visible cues: edge variation, physical overlap, paper scale, torn or cut logic,
registration offset, heterogeneous image sources held by one composition.

Emotional use: discovery, youth culture, contradiction, fast editorial intelligence.

Control: preserve source rights and distinguish quoted material from generated art.
Every fragment needs a role; random accumulation reads as a moodboard.

## Color dramaturgy

Use color as a sequence of forces:

- **field** — the color condition the viewer enters;
- **counterforce** — the color that interrupts or accelerates the field;
- **neutral** — the value that stabilizes reading and identity;
- **signal** — optional, small, and purposeful.

The field should dominate. A 50/50 distribution of brand colors often feels like a
template or team uniform. Ratios may change by format, but role and contrast logic
should remain recognizable.

## Controlled imperfection

Use imperfections at the point where the imagined medium would produce them:

- ink spread at edges or heavy deposits;
- wear at contact or traffic zones;
- scan tears along a transmission axis;
- drips following gravity;
- halftone changing with tonal value;
- blur following camera or object motion;
- shadow following the actual object and light source.

If one texture can be toggled off without changing the idea, it is probably polish,
not the mechanism. Treat it accordingly and never use it to rescue a weak composition.
