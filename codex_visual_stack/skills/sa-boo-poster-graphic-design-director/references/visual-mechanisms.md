# Visual Mechanisms

Read this when a poster feels assembled rather than authored, when references are
plentiful but no visual engine is clear, or when composition studies need a sharper
decision.

## A mechanism is a relationship, not a motif

Basketballs, stars, court lines, shoes, arrows, halftone, and graffiti are motifs.
They become useful only when they participate in a visible cause-and-effect
relationship.

```text
weak: large headline + court line + star + texture
mechanism: a single court diagonal compresses the headline until the words become a collision
```

Name the mechanism with a verb. If it cannot be expressed as an action or spatial
condition, it is probably a list of ingredients.

## Evidence decoding

For each selected reference, record:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| attraction | What is perceived first at thumbnail scale? |
| effect | What emotion, tempo, or meaning follows? |
| mechanism | Which scale, crop, overlap, axis, material, or type relationship causes it? |
| do-not-copy | What is recognizable as the source's authored expression? |
| original translation | What project-specific content can carry the same principle differently? |

Do not merge all mechanisms from all references. Select one dominant mechanism,
then borrow only supporting behaviors that strengthen it.

## Useful mechanism families

These are lenses, not presets.

### Field and collision

A large spatial or chromatic field is interrupted by one force: type, image, crop,
or a contrasting plane. Useful for confident campaign openers and brand statements.

Evidence to verify:

- one field clearly dominates the canvas;
- the collision changes the shape or reading of both participants;
- support elements remain subordinate.

### Type as mass

Headline scale, stacking, compression, crop, or repetition becomes the image.
Useful when the words themselves are the campaign's strongest cultural object.

Evidence to verify:

- the silhouette of the type is memorable before it is read;
- the manipulation supports cadence and meaning;
- important glyphs remain sufficiently legible for the context.

### Enclosure and totem

Type and graphic geometry resolve into one closed emblem, badge, or symbolic object.
Useful for club identity moments, certificates, patches, or compact recognition.

Risk: generic sports-badge language. Require project-specific geometry, typographic
character, or cultural evidence; do not use it as the default poster answer.

### Site and material inscription

The message appears physically written, painted, printed, projected, torn, taped,
or worn into a real substrate. Useful for street, community, event, or place-led
communication.

Evidence to verify:

- perspective, light, edge, absorption, shadow, and wear belong to the substrate;
- the site's imperfection changes the typography;
- the result is not a clean vector lockup pasted onto a wall photo.

### Signal and transmission

Glow, scan, grid, distortion, fragmentation, or temporal trails communicate a
digital broadcast or unstable signal. Useful for motion, game-day, night, and
technology-led energy.

Risk: generic esports. Require a campaign-specific transmission idea and control
the number of effects.

### Diagram transformed into atmosphere

Court, map, path, score, orbit, or tactical geometry becomes spatial atmosphere
rather than an infographic. Useful when domain structure can hold a poetic or
cinematic reading.

Risk: meaningless ellipses and pseudo-technical decoration. Use only verified
geometry or a clearly declared abstraction.

### Image interruption

A photograph or illustration is cut, masked, overprinted, sequenced, solarized,
or partly withheld so that absence becomes active. Useful when imagery is strong
but a literal full-bleed photo would feel like advertising stock.

Evidence to verify:

- the interruption adds meaning or rhythm;
- the subject remains correctly represented;
- the treatment survives the smallest intended format.

## Selecting the dominant event

Rank candidate mechanisms against five questions:

1. Can it be recognized at thumbnail scale?
2. Does it express the campaign idea rather than merely the category?
3. Can real copy and brand assets live inside it without patches?
4. Can it recompose across required formats?
5. Does it leave room for an original project-specific translation?

Choose the mechanism with the strongest combined answer, not the one with the
most visual effects.

## Composition-study discipline

Inside one territory, vary only meaningful spatial decisions:

- dominant scale and crop;
- axis and reading direction;
- type-to-field contact;
- amount and location of negative space;
- image interruption or reveal;
- foreground/background reversal.

Keep concept, copy, brand authority, and dominant mechanism constant. If those
change, it is a new territory and belongs upstream.
