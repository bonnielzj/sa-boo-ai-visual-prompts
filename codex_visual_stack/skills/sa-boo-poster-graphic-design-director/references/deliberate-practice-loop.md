# Deliberate poster practice and aesthetic accumulation

Use this only for repeated exercises, reconstruction studies, or an explicitly
maintained aesthetic-learning program. The output is evidence for learning; it is
not automatically a finished campaign poster.

## Unit of learning

For a daily program that includes interior, branding, illustration, art direction,
or motion, choose the medium and its responsible discipline from the project goal
before invoking this poster module. Consider communication, image-making, type
and information, composition and colour, material and light, spatial use, and
motion where relevant. Select the capabilities that serve this work; do not load
every skill or force every visual element into the result. Typography can be the
principal event, a supporting voice, or absent, according to the actual purpose.

Inspect the latest three actual previews for repeated subjects, dominant events,
compositions, typographic roles, and construction methods. If recent work keeps
defaulting to lettering or the same engine, select a project-grounded event led by
another capability before making the next image. Changing words, palette, texture,
topic, or the medium's label is insufficient. Preserve continuity when the user
explicitly requests development of a selected version. Visible variety must serve
content; a medium rotation alone is not evidence of progress.

Within that project decision, each practice session tests one unresolved visual
relationship while judging the complete work, such as:

- headline and image sharing one spatial action;
- logo participating in the dominant event or information hierarchy;
- colour area and edge behaviour creating depth or pressure;
- material treatment having a credible visual cause;
- functional information belonging to a boundary, field, object, or rhythm.

Do not call a new topic, medium, colour palette, effect, prompt, or completed file
package progress by itself. Progress requires a mechanism that can be identified,
reproduced under known conditions, transferred, and updated by actual feedback.

Integration does not require gigantic type, physical 3D interlocking, or a repeated
letter-container frame. It can arise from photographic focus, optical boundaries,
rhythm, color behavior, negative space, sequence, or semantic dependence. Select
the relationship that serves this topic; do not convert one partial success into
the default visual engine for later exercises.

## Two-stage study

### A. Reconstruction

Choose one strong traceable reference for the unresolved relationship. Inspect the
actual image at full and thumbnail scale. Reconstruct the relevant hierarchy,
proportion, overlap, rhythm, colour distribution, type-image relationship, and
identity/information role closely enough to test whether the visible mechanism was
understood. Do not substitute a list of prompt keywords or a loosely similar mood.

Compare the study beside the reference. Record where the relationship matches,
where it collapses, and what was merely copied on the surface. A weak reconstruction
cannot support a claimed validated transfer of that relationship. Repair or choose
another viable relationship when appropriate; an unfinished full-reference study
is not a blanket reason to stop an explicitly requested daily project design.

### B. Translation

After reconstruction is credible, change the subject or theme while preserving the
tested relationship. Redesign project-specific form, wording, identity, colour, and
content. If the mechanism disappears after translation, keep it `UNVALIDATED` rather
than claiming originality or progress.

Judge two different results: whether the studied relationship survives, and whether
the new work serves its project purpose with an engaging whole composition. Success
in the first does not certify the second. Do not keep forcing literal contact,
interlocking, or embedded captions into every translation; the required connection
can be compositional, perceptual, semantic, or temporal. A partly accepted dimension
remains available under its own conditions, not as a permanent style template.

Use one output when one mechanism is being tested. Create a second only for a real
controlled comparison; hold the important conditions fixed and change one named
relationship.

## Logo and functional-information roles

Do not force a logo, greeting, date, weekday, slogan, or editorial module into every
study. A pure composition, typography, colour, or material study may omit them and
remain explicitly `UNBRANDED_STUDY`.

For a branded poster, choose the logo's role before composing:

- `HERO_FORM` — the supplied identity becomes the principal spatial or graphic form;
- `STRUCTURAL_PARTNER` — its outline, negative space, scale, edge, alignment, crop,
  repetition, or material relation actively organizes the dominant event;
- `CERTIFICATION` — a quieter signature with a deliberate place in the information
  hierarchy and shared axes, spacing, contrast logic, or field behaviour.

Correct source artwork and clear legibility are necessary but do not prove
integration. Reject a logo that is pasted into leftover space, isolated in an
unmotivated white box or patch, or grouped into a tidy module that has no visual
relationship with the poster. Do not distort the supplied identity to fake a
relationship; if no role works, return to composition or omit the logo from the
study instead of forcing a nominal finished poster.

## Evidence and persistent learning

Link each study to the exact reference IDs, output version, tested mechanism, and
user feedback. Maintain a method ledger with:

```text
method_id / visual relationship
reference evidence
reconstruction asset and result
translation asset and result
user feedback and scope
state: UNTESTED / FAILED / PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED / USER_ACCEPTED / TRANSFER_VALIDATED
conditions, failure mode, and next retrieval action
```

User acceptance is a high-value positive label; rejection is a negative label;
silence and technical PASS are not positive evidence. A partial success remains
scoped to the accepted dimension. Promote a method to the domain skill only after
it survives a relevant transfer or repeated evidence.

At the next session, retrieve the unresolved method and its scoped positive and
negative evidence, rather than automatically reusing the last reference or its
resulting look. When the user flags converging styles or construction methods,
identify the repeated engine in recent previews and choose a traceable reference
with a different dominant event, medium or typographic role before the new
translation. A palette change alone does not answer that correction. Preserve
continuity when she explicitly asks to continue a selected series or version.
Accumulation should improve selection and prediction, not just file count.

## Stop and delivery rule

Show one standard-resolution preview as soon as possible. Before presenting it as
the day's poster, compare it with the chosen reference, the method hypothesis, and
the nearest recent failures. If it repeats a known failure—mechanical logo, detached
type, presentation-slide modules, generic effects, or a weak imitation—make a
targeted repair. If the central relationship still fails, save that version as
`NOT_DELIVERED_LEARNING_FAILURE`, then change the construction method or
reference relationship and make another purposeful internal attempt. Do not let
a fixed one-repair cap end the session while a viable route remains; also do not
repeat the same failed prompt indefinitely. Present an improved preview as a
review-pending study, without calling it approved or a finished poster.

When repeated outputs are rejected and the user asks for more learning, strengthen
authored-reference inspection and controlled reconstruction within the current
brief. Her latest instruction determines whether the task is research-only or
research plus daily design; dissatisfaction alone is not a permanent stop on
creation. When she explicitly requests a daily design, research must inform that
actual deliverable rather than silently replace it. Do not answer rejection by
merely changing the topic or adding a slogan to another generic scene. Research
materials must be labeled as research, not presented as original design.

A credible local reconstruction supports only the relationship actually tested.
It does not validate an unrelated semantic claim, the whole composition,
typographic voice, or a new photographic subject. Verify the actual artwork
against title, creator, date and source; an article can discuss several works
while linking a different image. Use controlled removals or one-variable
comparisons when they can disprove a proposed necessary condition. Preserve
counterexamples and uncompleted full-layout work before original translation.

Preserve only useful assets: the preview, source/reference link, prompt or editable
source when it supports revision, and a concise ledger update. Do not make SVG,
contact sheets, manifests, QA reports, or multiple decorative variants mandatory.
