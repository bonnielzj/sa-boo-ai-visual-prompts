# Format Adaptation and Safe Zones

Read this when one approved poster or KV must work across vertical, square,
landscape, feed, story, screen, print, or motion-hold formats.

## Lock recognition before adaptation

Define the invariant campaign signature:

```text
dominant visual event
headline voice and key line break
field/counterforce color roles
one project-specific graphic or image relationship
identity role and certification behavior
```

Formats may change crop, scale, arrangement, and amount of supporting information.
They must not replace the invariant with a generic template.

## Classify the formats

Group requested outputs by compositional behavior rather than listing dimensions:

- `VERTICAL_STORY` — long reading path, interface overlays, mobile thumb;
- `VERTICAL_POSTER` — distance viewing, physical or high-resolution digital poster;
- `SQUARE_FEED` — compressed opening event and limited detail density;
- `LANDSCAPE_SCREEN` — lateral movement, lower safe area, presentation or LED use;
- `WIDE_BANNER` — short dwell time and severe vertical compression;
- `PRINT_LARGE` — physical distance, bleed, substrate, vendor constraints;
- `MOTION_HOLD` — frame-safe title and a visual event that can animate without losing identity.

Use named delivery dimensions supplied by the user or channel. Do not invent a full
matrix when only one format is requested.

## Recompose, do not shrink

For each format, decide:

- which edge can crop the dominant field;
- which type line or image feature may move;
- what must remain visible at thumbnail or distance;
- what information can move to a caption or secondary frame;
- whether the logo uses a verified alternate arrangement;
- where platform overlays, trim, fold, or screen obstructions occur.

Do not solve an aspect-ratio change by scaling the entire poster, adding empty bands,
or reducing all content until it fits.

## Safe zones

Maintain three different boundaries:

1. **production boundary** — trim, bleed, screen edge, or export frame;
2. **platform/obstruction safe zone** — interface overlays, crop uncertainty, captions,
   notches, or venue hardware;
3. **semantic safe zone** — area in which decisive glyphs, faces, marks, dates, and
   the campaign anchor cannot be unintentionally cut.

The dominant visual event may cross the semantic safe zone intentionally; mandatory
meaning may not. Oversized type can bleed while its decisive character forms remain
recognizable.

## Proof requirements

For each requested class, review:

- full frame at intended scale;
- thumbnail or distance simulation;
- crop with platform/production overlays;
- copy and logo legibility;
- continuity of the dominant event;
- whether supporting texture or microtype survives or should be removed.

A variant is not successful merely because all content fits. It must feel composed
for that format and remain recognizably part of the same campaign.
