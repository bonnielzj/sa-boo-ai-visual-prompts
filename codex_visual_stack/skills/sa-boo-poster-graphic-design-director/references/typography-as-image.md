# Typography as Image

Read this when typography must carry the poster, bilingual copy needs an authored
relationship, or a candidate relies on a generic font plus effects.

## Begin with voice and action

Define each text role by voice and behavior before choosing a face:

```text
headline: command / collision / compressed forward mass
English echo: mnemonic / rhythmic counterline / lower semantic weight
details: factual / calm / stable reading rail
identity: verified mark / certification, not decoration
```

Font category alone is not direction. A forceful poster can come from grotesk,
serif, script, slab, condensed, expanded, vernacular, or custom lettering when
scale, spacing, silhouette, and spatial behavior support the idea.

## Behaviors that can turn type into an image

Use only those justified by the dominant mechanism:

- **scale and crop** — letters exceed the frame and create a memorable edge;
- **stack and compression** — lines become one dense mass with controlled rhythm;
- **collision and interruption** — type meets a field, object, cut, or rule and is
  visibly changed by it;
- **repetition and cadence** — a word or fragment creates tempo, echo, or buildup;
- **axis and path** — type follows a credible structural force rather than a random
  diagonal;
- **figure/ground reversal** — counters, knockouts, or masks reveal a second field;
- **weight contrast** — a heavy semantic voice is paired with a precise factual
  voice;
- **custom intervention** — selected terminals, joins, cuts, or ligatures answer the
  campaign mechanism while preserving glyph integrity.

Drop shadow, outline, bevel, extrusion, or glow may support a behavior but cannot
substitute for one.

## Chinese and English roles

Determine semantic authority from the brief. For a Chinese parent or youth audience,
Chinese usually carries the core proposition; English can reinforce memory, tempo,
or international tone. Reverse that only when the actual audience and campaign
require it.

Avoid:

- English filler that says nothing and merely occupies space;
- direct translation repeated at equal scale when it creates two competing headlines;
- forcing Chinese into a white rectangle because the art has no contrast plan;
- using unrelated display faces whose moods conflict;
- shrinking Chinese details below real mobile or print reading conditions;
- fake Latin glyph treatment applied mechanically to Chinese anatomy.

Coordinate the two scripts through compatible contrast, density, stress, terminal
energy, width, and cadence—not by demanding that they look identical.

## Typography discovery gate

Use an approved project face when its role still fits. When the choice is genuinely
unresolved, inspect a small relevant set of installed or licensed CJK and Latin
faces using the actual copy. Do not rescan the full font pool or make specimen
boards for every preview. Font novelty cannot resolve a weak message or hierarchy.
This is discovery by **role**, not a fixed font prescription.

For the actual copy, test a small set against roles such as:

- impact headline mass;
- expressive editorial contrast;
- athletic/vernacular cadence;
- condensed factual rail;
- English mnemonic echo;
- highly readable Chinese information face.

Compare actual glyph rhythm, width, counter shape, terminal energy, punctuation,
numerals, weight range, and relationship between scripts. Local availability does
not imply suitability, and unusual letterforms do not excuse weak Chinese reading.

Do not download a font unless its source and license are verified for the intended
use. Do not redistribute, embed, or outline-convert a font to evade license terms.
If licensing remains uncertain, keep it out of the production master and record a
licensed fallback.

## Typeface evidence

Before committing to a master:

1. inspect the actual installed/local font or licensed source;
2. verify required Chinese glyphs, punctuation, numerals, and weights;
3. test the real headline, not a specimen word;
4. compare at output scale and thumbnail scale;
5. record the font file or family and fallback;
6. keep licensing and embedding requirements explicit.

Do not infer that a font is available because it appears in a screenshot or a
reference. When Figma or another tool substitutes a font, treat the result as a
layout proof, not the final typographic master.

## Hierarchy as rhythm

Poster hierarchy is not a stack of font sizes. Build a reading rhythm:

```text
impact beat → mnemonic echo → factual pause → certification
```

Use position, density, silence, line break, alignment, color, and material as well
as size. Detail copy should be readable but need not compete with the opening beat.

## Legibility under expressive treatment

Test the actual use case:

- 3-second mobile glance for social;
- street or lobby distance for a physical poster;
- cropped preview in a feed;
- small repost or parent-group image;
- motion hold frame if used in video.

Preserve recognition of decisive Chinese characters, dates, age groups, and action
information. Expressive illegibility may be acceptable for a secondary texture or
echo, never for mandatory information.

## Signs of generic type treatment

Rework when:

- the same font, shadow, and centered stack could carry any sports brand;
- the headline is surrounded by decorative objects but does not affect them;
- hierarchy depends on a card or patch rather than composition;
- outlines multiply to compensate for a weak silhouette;
- English is the most visually interesting line but Chinese contains the actual idea;
- letter spacing, line breaks, and punctuation remain default software decisions.
