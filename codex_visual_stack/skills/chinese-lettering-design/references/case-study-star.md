# Character recognition within a graphic wordmark

These are generic construction examples, not a record of a client project or an
approved visual style. They illustrate two independent checks.

## Whole-word composition

Two individually modified characters may still read as separate typeset letters
when the brief asks for a single graphic wordmark. Before adding effects, inspect
the combined silhouette, direction, shared negative spaces and rhythm. A shared
sweep or compatible interlock can connect the composition when appropriate to the
actual text. Additional spikes, texture or orbit text cannot repair an unrelated
underlying structure.

## Correct counters

The 日 component has two interior counters separated by a horizontal stroke.
A white closed ring containing an isolated black island can resemble that component
from far away while having different topology. Inspect both counters and the
cross-stroke at useful scale. If an island is unintended, reconnect or redraw the
contour while matching the selected stroke direction and weight. Check nearby
components separately; a plausible whole silhouette does not prove every character.

Compare the repaired contours with the exact requested character sequence, then
view the complete wordmark at its intended size. OCR confidence, a trace-overlap
score, path counts and vector-only structure each address limited questions; none
establishes correct anatomy or aesthetic success.

## Secondary copy and application

For copy on an ellipse or another path, preserve the supplied wording and inspect
the seam, spacing and perspective together. Small text may function as texture at
reduced size; that does not establish readability in embroidery or print. Actual
physical dimensions and supplier/sample evidence are needed for such conclusions.

Keep useful editable sources in the active project's own record. A generic example
is neither an existing destination to edit nor authorization to reuse another
project's content.
