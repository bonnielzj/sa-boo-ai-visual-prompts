# Lettering construction and motion

## Turn a reference into decisions

Use a short working record, not a mandatory presentation page:

| Question | Useful decision |
|---|---|
| What reads first? | A compact mass, spreading wings, rising diagonal, soft loop, heavy block or dispersed field. |
| What changes in the strokes? | Width contrast, curvature, compression, shared edges, directional taper, interruption. |
| What stays stable? | The recognition anchors of each actual Chinese character. |
| What does the white space do? | Two internal cells, a channel between components, a crescent, wedge or large open counter. |
| Why this mechanism? | Its visible behavior supports the requested meaning and use. |
| What is merely finish? | Grain, glow, color, transparency, simulated material. |

Record one primary mechanism and the feature that will make its success visible. “Dynamic” becomes a rising shared axis, tapered terminals or a traveling compression field. “Like a graphic” becomes a unified outline, interlocked masses and deliberate negative channels. Do not claim that every word must use the same vocabulary.

## Chinese recognition before abstraction

Write the exact string separately from the artwork. Preserve simplified/traditional and regional choices. Do not silently normalize a character variant. Identify vulnerable components with a reliable type specimen or character source; a tracing or generated image is not that source.

For each difficult character, note a few anchors rather than enforcing conventional proportions everywhere. Examples:

- 日 needs a divided enclosure; a white ring around a floating black island is not equivalent.
- 斗 needs its two dots in the correct relation to its crossing strokes; decorative symmetry can put one on the wrong side.
- 土/士, 未/末, 口/日/目 and 天/夫 depend on specific relative lengths or crossings. Do not let a repeated style rule erase their distinction.
- Dots and short strokes can become slices, droplets or wedges, but their number/position may carry recognition.

Keep a reasonably ordinary specimen beside a highly abstract drawing during construction. This is a check, not a requirement to preserve standard font boxes or forbid expressive distortion. Review ambiguous results with a second reader when useful; name uncertainty rather than inventing a reading.

## Construct a graphic wordmark

1. Draw a small black mass sketch for the whole word. Decide its maximum reach, dense core, center of gravity and dominant direction. Treat the outer contour as part of the lettering, not a background badge.
2. Allocate optical space to the characters. A complex character may need a larger open counter or thinner internal supports; equal bounding boxes often produce unequal density.
3. Choose where characters can nest. A protruding stroke can enter an adjacent recess; a long horizontal can support both characters. Do not reduce all spacing until letters crash or leave an accidental upright gap through the entire word.
4. Draw the major positive masses and the negative channels together. Test the white shapes independently: they should be intentional, varied enough to identify parts, and consistent with the curve language.
5. Develop tapered terminals as continuations of the contours. Avoid pasted triangles, arbitrary lightning bolts or detached stars unless the brief specifically calls for them.
6. Refine Bezier handles, transitions and optical weight. Unite overlapping fills when practical, preserving meaningful holes. Remove tracing fragments only after confirming they are not punctuation or semantic dots.

Connected lettering does not require every black region to touch. A convincing graphic can contain separate dots and counters. The goal is coherent visual organization, not a single connected-component count.

## Metaphor in the glyphs

Choose operations from the requested meaning, not generic decorations:

| Meaning or behavior | Possible structural operation | Review risk |
|---|---|---|
| Burning / melting | Preserve an upper recognition zone; stretch selected lower contours with gravity; vary drip lengths and widths. | Uniform drips erase the glyph; glow alone does not make it melt. |
| Smoke / disappearance | Gradually thin, open or fragment noncritical ends along a chosen direction; keep a clean underlying version. | Noise may remove critical components or become unreadable at small scale. |
| New possibility / open sky | Widen selected counters, release a terminal into open space, let the group rise or expand. | A pasted star icon can replace rather than express the letterform. |
| Tension / speed | Slanted axis, compressed core, stretched exits and consistent tapered negative wedges. | Simple global italics can leave ordinary font anatomy unchanged. |
| Softness / intimacy | Curved shared strokes, open loops, rhythm between broad and narrow spaces. | Decorative swashes can dominate or cross the recognition zones. |
| Mass / impact | Thick block forms shaped by carved internal channels. | Complex characters become solid black when scaled down. |

These are options, not automatic translations. Read the selected reference before choosing one.

## Static motion is not animation

A printed product can imply motion through geometry: rotation along a curve, width/height modulation, changing slope, displaced bands, extension in a consistent direction, and a clear progression of spacing or mass. Use one coherent motion field. Random per-character rotations and arbitrary size changes usually produce noise.

For an orbit of tiny repeated text:

- Repeat the exact supplied copy with explicit separators; keep an editable source string.
- Place by arc length, not equal angle on an ellipse. Equal angular steps crowd the tight ends.
- Align each glyph to the local tangent. A continuously clockwise ring naturally turns upside down on its lower half. Do not flip half the ring unless a deliberately two-direction reading arrangement is wanted.
- Let size or width vary smoothly to suggest near/far. Adjust spacing using the actual advances, including full-width spaces. Close the cycle without a doubled word, hidden glyph or abrupt gap.
- Prevent secondary text from piercing recognition anchors or shrinking the hero into insignificance. A partial occlusion can express depth if deliberate; an accidental collision cannot.
- At product size, record whether the copy remains readable. If it only forms a texture, say so; do not call it readable microtype.

For “motion in the type itself,” inspect individual contours as well as the path. Orbit placement and size variation provide orbital motion, but they do not themselves create sheared, stretched or hand-designed glyph anatomy. If the user wants that stronger effect, redesign/warp the actual glyphs while retaining their recognition anchors.

## Effects and output size

Keep a clean silhouette when an effect-heavy treatment may later need adaptation. Latin noise or halftone examples transfer an effect field, not Chinese component topology. Do not automatically trace every grain into thousands of paths.

If an artwork is W pixels wide and its intended ink width is M mm, an observed detail d pixels wide corresponds to d×M/W mm. A font's em size is not its visible ink height. These are scale estimates, not process limits. Validate the chosen process with its actual material, supplier and sample when that phase is requested.
