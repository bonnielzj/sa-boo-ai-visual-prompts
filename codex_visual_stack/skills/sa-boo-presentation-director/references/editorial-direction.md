# Editorial direction for commercial portfolios

Use this when the user requests a premium case book or rejects layout, type,
image selection, or a shallow reading of a reference. The outcome is a credible
commercial document with authored visual pacing, not a checklist-shaped deck.

## Recall and inspect

Inspect a small representative selection of the supplied references relevant to
the current sample: a cover, contents, case opener, image spread, detail spread or
dense spread as needed. Read fonts and proportions where practical. Recall earlier
project research only when it resolves a real gap or preserves an approved element;
keep proposed, approved and rejected work distinct. Do not inherit another brand's
identity.

Describe the mechanism that matters: image scale relative to type, crop and
focal position, bilingual roles, margins, baseline relationships and sequencing.
Then make a project-specific translation. 'Black and white', 'minimal' and
'luxury' are not sufficient design specifications.

## Establish the system on real content

Use the same real project material to test the page types needed for the current
sample, such as a cover, case opener, product/detail spread or evidence/service
page. After fundamental rejection, compare two
genuinely different compositions before replication; do not vary only color.
Show the actual rendered pages. Do not call either approved without user selection.
Continue independent research and editing while feedback is pending.

The proof should resolve:

- Type: display personality, Chinese reading voice, Latin navigation, captions;
  actual strings, line breaks, tracking and optical alignment, not placeholder text.
- Scale: an unmistakable primary subject; supporting images earn their space.
  Use full or near-full-page photography when the source supports it.
- Rhythm: change field of view, density and reading speed. A useful sequence may
  move from scene to object to material to use, then to business interpretation.
  This is an example, not a mandatory layout rotation.
- Graphics: use rules, numerals, overlaps or apertures only with a compositional
  role. Avoid adding decoration merely to escape blandness.
- Evidence: develop each case beyond a product list—brief, design decisions,
  outputs, realized uses, and supported delivery scope. Distinguish plans,
  samples, retail evidence and social response without exposing private finance.

For a case book circulated to prospective clients, typography must leave the
work in command. Treat criticism of excessive serif, all-sans monotony, or
oversized type as criticism of the actual composition; do not reverse the deck
into another single-font extreme. A serif accent may give a chapter or tactile
feature personality while a sans-serif voice carries structure and Chinese
reading. An all-sans system can also work when the current brief supports it.
Choose by the rendered pairing and its role, not a universal font-count rule.

In a bilingual case book, English needs meaningful editorial content and a
visible compositional role. A tiny city or project code alone does not restore
bilingual hierarchy. Test the complete English and Chinese title pair for
measure, line breaks, baseline relationship and relative emphasis. Vary the
position with the imagery while keeping its reading roles recognizable.
When type feels too large or busy, revise its roles across the deck and remove
redundant copy before shrinking individual boxes. Review actual longest lines
for stray punctuation and single-character wraps.

When creative worlds have different tones, use an authored chapter bridge and
sequence instead of forcing equal-scale adjacency. Explain the source and the
translation with the minimum useful copy, then let each world speak in its own
style. Preserve song/lyric, drawing, and generated-derivative authorship separately.
A final object page needs a closing function; do not let the book simply stop.

Small gray text is still content. Remove lower-left fragments, redundant image
descriptions and decorative metadata that do not help a prospective client
understand the work. Keep source details in notes and QA records outside the
deck. When a screenshot is the evidence, give it enough space to read; a small
caption cannot compensate for an undersized original.

## Asset finish and release judgment

For a product-case preview, use the named and supplied sources for the current
sample first. Expand to a product-family inventory when comprehensive final
coverage is required or a specific coverage gap could change the deck; do not
inventory the local library for a bounded proof. Distinguish a hero image, whole
object, material detail, assortment and use scene as relevant. Extract the original
embedded images where available. When a source is already a composed board, use
exact native crops to recover complete product regions; rebuild its headings as
editable text only when the delivery contract promises editability. A small
full-board thumbnail does not demonstrate product coverage.
Inspect crop boundaries for clipped product silhouettes, leftover source labels
and unintended background blocks before repeating the layout. Retain a board
whole only when its composition or original evidence is itself the subject.

A product cutout is not automatically a finished image. Inspect shape, seams,
artwork, color, texture, contact shadows, edges, and joins at normal scale and 100%.
Repair the photographed presentation faithfully or choose a better source before
hero placement; do not hide a collapsed, twisted, or poorly lit object in a layout.

Inspect the actual logo asset on the intended background at normal size and
close-up. Use a verified vector/alpha source where possible. Do not replace a
mark with approximate live text, hide it in a white JPEG rectangle, or accept
fringing. Keep photography, generated art and public-source evidence distinct.

Compare the proof with representative source spreads at equal scale. A contact
sheet reveals repetition; full-size pages reveal type and image craft. If the
new page gives the image less authority or weaker hierarchy without a content
reason, revise it before expanding. Structural QA is separate from this judgment;
self-assigned aesthetic scores do not establish client approval.

## Method sources

These sources support the mechanisms above; they are not templates or client
approval. The interpretation is original and their imagery is not imported.

- [Anthropic frontend-design](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md):
  subject-led choices, active typography, meaningful structure, one focal event.
  The source is Apache 2.0 and UI-focused; transfer composition judgment only.
- [Pentagram — Pink Floyd Records](https://www.pentagram.com/work/pink-floyd-records):
  authored recognition and orderly archival presentation can coexist with
  independent artwork. This never authorizes changing a supplied logo.
- [Pentagram — First Two Pages of Frankenstein](https://www.pentagram.com/work/first-two-pages-of-frankenstein/story):
  establish an album-specific idea, then develop its actual application range.
- [Butterick — headings](https://practicaltypography.com/headings.html) and
  [page margins](https://practicaltypography.com/page-margins.html): clarify reading
  structure and spacing, then judge optical results rather than numeric ratios.
